COUCY

the gate was open. mind the stairs, there is no handrail.

Enguerrand III began the castle around 1225 and put a cylinder at the corner of it that nobody has matched since: 55 metres tall, 31 across, walls seven and a half metres thick. Three vaulted halls stacked on top of each other, no internal buttressing, no keystone bigger than it needed to be. It was not the strongest castle in France. It was the loudest.

Ground level. The door is nine metres up the outside — you came in the way a guest would, which is to say by a bridge someone could pull away.

March 1917. Falling back to the Hindenburg Line, German sappers packed the donjon with explosive and brought it down. There had been no fighting at Coucy. The tower had stood for nearly seven hundred years, and what is on the hill now is a ring of rubble with the shape still legible in it.
