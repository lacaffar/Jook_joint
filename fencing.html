react in the called direction before the window closes - W/A/D, arrows, or the buttons. in foil & sabre right-of-way decides whether you're attacking or parrying. watch for feints.

Fencing is physical chess with a side of adrenaline.

Right-of-way, distance, timing, and the nerve to commit - all settled in fractions of a second under a buzzer and two coloured lights. It rewards patience and punishes panic.

The weapons, quickly

  • Foil - torso, right-of-way
  • Épée - whole body, no priority
  • Sabre - cuts & thrusts, fast

What I'd add to this bout next

  • Distance as a real axis - advance and retreat, so the strip is something you fight over instead of a backdrop.
  • An opponent that learns: punish the same parry three times and it should start feinting into it.
  • Show the right-of-way call after each touch, so the rule teaches itself.
